How to use it

  • Drag the distance slider to go anywhere on the road, or click either strip. Play runs the trip in time, red lights included; dragging jumps over them, because a stop has no length on the road.
  • Judge the feel at 1×. At higher speeds the narration and the catch-up fade run fast too, which is fine for finding places but not for judging fades.
  • Change the zone widths and the whole trip is recomputed from the start: skips, blocked fade-ins and crossfades all move. The figures under the strips are for comparing settings.
  • The corner shows distance, trip share, age and speed. The bottom panel shows the event being narrated, how far its narration has got, and the journey bar.
  • Pictures are placeholders: the event's real heading and date on the §K.3 colour at its position. The text is deliberately low-contrast, the plate's own colour a little lighter or darker, so that like a real card it only reads once the picture is fully there.
  • Time marks come every 0.1 Ga (every 10 Ma below 100 Ma), each passing at its exact point on the road. They fly in the tunnel, so an event picture covers any that fall inside its zones.

Interpretations still mine, for your ruling

  1. Narration is heading plus body only, as in §M. If the TTS also reads the spoken date first, every narration is about two seconds longer.
  2. Ignition: 1.5 s of black, the first picture fades up over 3 s, narration starts at 4.5 s, and the car pulls away at 5 s.
  3. No-filler gap, fade-in blocked part-way: once the earlier picture has started fading out by road, it keeps fading; it does not jump back to full when the next one resumes.
  4. Colour follows §K.3 (hue 355° → 195°, 75% index / 25% distance, lightness rising) at a constant chroma. The physics chroma profile is not modelled yet. Earth & Life has no ramp of its own yet, so it borrows the Universe one.
  5. Earth & Life formulas (decay law, half-life, uranium–lead, potassium–argon, photosynthesis, respiration, iron rusting out of the sea, chalk, acid sea, the carbon fingerprint, DNA pairs) are a first set for your editorial pass (§K.8). Names float too (Earth & Life): eons, eras, periods, epochs, glaciations, continents and oceans, and species in italics, without explanations. A name floats only while it is true at your position on the road; formulas float anywhere.
